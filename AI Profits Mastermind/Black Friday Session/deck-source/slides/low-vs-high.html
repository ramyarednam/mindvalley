Low ticket vs high ticket

Low ticket sells on the deal. High ticket sells on the decision.

Low ticket · under ~$500High ticket · $2K+
Price moveDiscount, bundle, buy-one-get-oneBonuses, extra access, payment plan
Window24–72 hours, open to allWaitlist early access, then doors
Who buysYour whole list + warm adsPast clients + engaged warm leads
How they buyOne-click checkoutApplication, call or deposit
ScarcityPrice ends SundaySeats tied to your capacity
Job in the ladderFills the roomPays the bills

Black Friday Launch Engine · 9

Slide 9 · starts 15:00 · full words in the script doc. Key points: Same Black Friday, two different games. Low ticket (courses, templates, memberships, workshops): the buyer decides alone in minutes, so a clear deal, a short window and one-click checkout win. Discounts and bundles work; Amy Porterfield’s BOGO is the model. High ticket (coaching, masterminds, done-for-you): the buyer decides with their head and often a call, so never deep-discount. Add bonuses, extra access, a payment plan or deposit, and sell to warm people first through early access and a conversation. Low-ticket digital products carry Black Friday discounts well, while high-ticket services underperform when discounted (Rebekah Read Creative).
