## 1. What Mindvalley is, and what this role owns

Mindvalley is a 22-year-old personal growth education company. It sells transformation — Quests (courses), Membership, Masteries, Certifications, Summits and live events — to a global consumer audience, with Vishen Lakhiani as the founder and primary voice.

**Email is not a support function here. It is a primary revenue channel.** A single lifecycle journey (Member Onboarding, L3) has produced **$1,436,316** in attributed revenue off 32,562 deliveries. A summit invite sequence reaches **5M+ sends** across segments in a single launch email.

### What the Email Lead owns

1. **The email calendar** — what goes out, to whom, when, in what language. Nothing ships that isn't on it.
2. **Copy quality and brand voice** — you are the last line between a draft and 5 million inboxes.
3. **The governance process** — you are the mandatory reviewer. No email goes live without your approval (§6).
4. **The daily broadcast slot** — the everyday send to the broadcast list.
5. **Campaign sequences** — Invite, Show-up, Sales for every summit/launch.
6. **Lifecycle journeys** — onboarding, reactivation, upsell, trial, churn (shared with the Lifecycle team).
7. **Performance reporting** — the weekly read, the MOW dashboard, campaign retros.

### What you do NOT own
- **Vishen's weekly newsletter — Ramya retains this.** See the carve-out below.
- Purely transactional email (receipts, confirmations)
- Cohort-start logistics emails
- Customer support comms
- Ad creative and paid media
- The platform/product roadmap

### The newsletter carve-out

Ramya continues to own Vishen's weekly newsletter after the handover — the sync, the draft, the distribution and the translation handoff. It is the one email that does not route through the Email Lead. Three things follow from that, and all three need saying out loud rather than discovering later:

1. **Review authority has one exception.** You are the mandatory reviewer for every email at Mindvalley *except* the newsletter, which Ramya owns and approves. Anyone unsure which applies should ask, not guess.
2. **The calendar still has one owner: you.** The newsletter occupies a slot on your calendar like anything else. When a campaign send and the newsletter collide on a day, segment or suppression list, you flag it and Ramya decides. This happens more than you'd expect — the Sep 7 Expert to Authority announcement and the Sep 9 newsletter ended up as one combined send across 18 segment-sends.
3. **Frequency capping is a shared problem.** The newsletter reaches nearly every segment weekly. Your campaign volume sits on top of it. Watch total weekly touches per segment, not just your own.

### 2.2 The person (the avatar)

Behind every list is one person. Internally we call her **Julie.**

- 38–52. Solopreneur, freelancer, consultant, coach, or small-team founder.
- Knows the topic matters. Feels behind. Working late. Kids growing up.
- Has bought courses she didn't finish. Carries guilt about that.
- Brilliant and exhausted. **Not technical.**
- **Not** exclusively an entrepreneur — 72% of the list are non-founders. A listicle aimed at "10 founders" lost its test for exactly this reason.

**The 7am test — apply it to every email you write:**

> Julie is making coffee. Her teenager just left for school. She has 47 emails. She sees yours.
> Would she open it? Read every word? Feel something? Click?
> If any sentence breaks that flow — cut it.

## 3. Mindvalley brand tone of voice

### 3.1 The core stance

Mindvalley emails are **one human writing to one human.** Usually that human is Vishen. The voice is:

- **Raw and personal** — a founder talking to a friend, not a brand broadcasting.
- **Story-first** — a scene, a confession, a specific moment. Never a feature list.
- **Outcome-led** — lead with what changes in her life, not with the mechanism.
- **Generous** — give a usable result *before* asking for anything.
- **Uncynical** — we genuinely believe people can change. No fear-based selling.

Real opening, from the Sep 2026 Expert to Authority sequence — this is the register to aim for:

> *"I was sitting at Soho House in Stockholm. I came to Stockholm to attend a longevity conference. I assembled people around me: founders, a few Mindvalley authors, close friends. I asked them an important question. I'd gathered them because I was confused. And I wanted the truth from people who wouldn't flatter me."*

Specific place. Specific discomfort. No adjectives doing the work. That's the voice.

### 3.2 The six principles every Mindvalley email is built on

| Principle | Source | In practice |
|---|---|---|
| **Starving Crowd** | Halbert | Don't convince her the problem matters — she knows. Show her this was built *specifically* for her. |
| **Slippery Slide** | Sugarman | Every element has one job: get the next sentence read. Short paragraphs. White space. |
| **Results in Advance** | Kern | Give a real, usable result before asking. A 90-second exercise she can do today. |
| **Sideways Sales Letter** | Walker | Each email is one chapter. By email 10 she registers because she's already changed a little. |
| **Emotional Resonance First** | Boggs | Connect emotionally *before* leading logically. Mirror her life, then offer the path. |
| **One Belief, Every Email** | Bari | Not 12 topics. One belief proven 12 ways. Compounding conviction. |

### 3.3 Julie's four emotional stages

Every email in a sequence must sit in exactly one stage:

1. **SEEN** (emails 1–2) — "Someone understands my life." No solutions yet. Just the mirror.
2. **HOPE** (emails 3–5) — "Maybe this time is different." Tiny wins. A student story. A 90-second demo.
3. **TRUST** (emails 6–8) — "These people know what they're doing." Method reveal, credibility, objection killing.
4. **COMMITTED** (emails 9–12) — Stop selling. Cement. Help her clear her calendar.

### 3.4 The locked rules

**NEVER say:**
- "Productivity" → say **"time freedom"** or **"free your Friday"**
- Fear of job loss, or AI replacing humans
- Jargon: MCP, n8n, agentic workflows, vibe coding, RAG
- "Master prompting" — creates false expectations
- "Entrepreneurs" or "founders" exclusively — include coaches, professionals, parents
- "Pitch" → always **"invitation"**
- "We're excited to announce…"
- "Don't miss this incredible opportunity!"
- More than one CTA in a single email

**ALWAYS:**
- "For the first time" language for genuine first-ever reveals
- Lead with outcomes, not mechanisms
- "No replays. No recordings." from email 7 onward on live events
- **One CTA per email**
- At least one specific name, number, or sensory detail
- 250–350 words for pre-event emails; **under 1,000 characters for WhatsApp**

### 3.5 The anatomy of a single email

1. **Subject line** — one job: get opened. *Would you text this to a friend?* If no, rewrite.
2. **First sentence** — short, unexpected, personal. A scene, a question, or a confession.
   - ✅ "I almost didn't send this email."
   - ✅ "Last Tuesday at 11pm, my phone buzzed."
   - ❌ "I'm excited to announce…"
3. **Story / scene** — 2–4 short paragraphs. Mirror, then shift.
4. **The bridge** — one sentence that reframes. Feels like an insight, not a lesson.
5. **CTA** — one action, framed as service: *"If this resonated, I made something for you."*

### 3.6 Pre-send checklist (run this every time)

- [ ] Reinforces the one core belief of this campaign?
- [ ] Passes the 7am test?
- [ ] Does sentence 1 create an itch to read sentence 2?
- [ ] No wall of text?
- [ ] Sounds like Vishen talking to a friend, not a campaign?
- [ ] Only ONE CTA?
- [ ] At least one specific name, number or sensory detail?
- [ ] Gives something before asking for something?
- [ ] Passes the locked rules (no productivity, no fear, no jargon)?
- [ ] Under 400 words (pre-event) or 1,000 characters (WhatsApp)?

## 4. How to come up with emails

This is the question everyone underestimates. Ideas are not invented at the keyboard — they are harvested from five standing sources.

### 4.1 The five idea sources

1. **Message of the Week (MOW)** — the Monday 16:30 meeting sets the company's single message for the week. Every channel — email, social, LinkedIn, YouTube, WhatsApp — aligns behind it. Your week's emails start here. Notes live in the MOW doc and the MOW Airtable.
2. **Vishen directly** — the weekly Newsletter Sync and the Strategy Deep Dive. His raw stories are the highest-performing asset we have. Capture them verbatim; do not smooth them out.
3. **The campaign calendar** — every summit/launch has a fixed sequence architecture (§5.4). The slots are known months ahead; you are filling known slots, not inventing structure.
4. **Performance data** — last week's winners tell you what angle to run again. See §8.
5. **The audience's own words** — summit transcripts, onboarding surveys, support tickets, NPS verbatims. The Onboarding Survey and summit transcript analyses in Drive are full of Julie's exact phrasing. Steal it.

### 4.2 The subject-line formulas

Seven repeatable patterns (Halbert's):

1. **The Confession** — "I wasted 3 years doing AI wrong"
2. **The Specific Number** — "2 hours. That's what changed."
3. **The Pattern Interrupt** — "I photographed my fridge. Then something weird happened."
4. **The "You" Mirror** — "Still checking email at your kid's game?"
5. **The Reframe** — "Stop trying to learn AI."
6. **The Whisper** — "quick question" / "something I almost didn't share"
7. **The Social Proof Surge** — "150,000 founders. 3 days. No replays."

**Proven winners:** "Big Freakin' Announcement (Open Up)" · "Is ChatGPT really everything AI has to offer?" · "Free Your Friday" · "OPEN: To unlock a 22-year old secret"

**Proven losers — never reuse:** "The 4-letter F word that still raises eyebrows" (list has seen it) · "Don't let AI overwhelm you" (negative framing) · **any subject line reused from a prior year — the list remembers.**

### 4.3 What we've learned the hard way

| Approach | Result | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| "Free Your Friday" — raw Vishen voice + an action (take the oath) | **+98%** | Active participation = identity shift = conversion |
| The "Possibilities Mirror" email | **+25%** | Mirror principle. No persuasion needed. |
| Agenda email (specific outcomes) | baseline | Specificity works but doesn't excite |
| Generic social proof quotes | **−64%** | Wrong proof — needs program-specific stories |
| Pain/survey angle | lost test | She already knows the problem |
| Listicle ("10 founders") | lost test | 72% of the list are non-founders |

**The single biggest lever:** when Vishen gives people something to *do*, they become active participants rather than passive recipients. Give the reader an action inside the email.

### 4.4 The reciprocity device

Give a usable exercise *inside* the email. The reader produces their own conversion argument. Example (the Freedom Audit):

> 1. Write down every task you did last week.
> 2. Next to each, write H (human only), A (AI could do this), or B (between).
> 3. Count your A's and B's. Estimate the hours. That's your Freedom Number.

Never explain why it works. Just give it.

### 5.5 Step-by-step: getting one email live

1. **Create the Airtable record** under Emails. Mandatory fields — all of them, or it won't be reviewed:
   Campaign Name · Email Type (Broadcast / Automation / Lifecycle / Campaign) · Target Audience · Objective (Revenue / Engagement / Show-up / Retention) · Subject Line · Preview Text · Email Copy · CTA · Send Date & Time · Owner
2. **Set status → "Ready for Review."** This notifies the reviewer in Slack.
3. **Review.** The reviewer checks messaging clarity, campaign alignment, brand voice, conversion optimisation, CTA clarity, segmentation relevance. Outcome is **Approved** or **Changes Required**.
4. **Status → "Approved for Execution."** *Only now* can the email be built in the ESP, assets finalised, scheduling begun.
5. **Build in Braze.** QA: links, personalization tokens, segmentation, tracking (UTMs + both otags), formatting on mobile *and* desktop.
6. **Status → Scheduled**, then **Live** once sent.
7. **Log it in the master tracker** (§8.1) — date, day, campaign, sequence, email name, send time, segment, suppression list, UTM link, Braze campaign link, subject line.

**Suppression discipline:** always suppress people who already converted. For the Expert to Authority WhatsApp DM the audience was explicitly "non-members and members who did not click on the invite emails or the Sep 9 newsletter — suppress the summit sign-ups." Sending a registration invite to someone who already registered is the fastest way to burn trust and inflate unsubs.

## 6. The Governance SOP — the non-negotiables

This is the process Ramya wrote and enforced. You inherit it, and you inherit the authority behind it.

> 🚫 **NO EMAIL GOES LIVE WITHOUT REVIEW STATUS APPROVED.**
> If it is not in Airtable → it does not exist.
> If it is not reviewed → it does not go live.

**Status flow:** `Draft → Ready for Review → Approved → In Execution → Scheduled → Live`
No skipping stages. No direct Draft → Live.

**The five rules:**

1. **Airtable is the source of truth.** No Slack approvals. No WhatsApp approvals. No verbal approvals.
2. **Review is mandatory.** If it's not marked Approved, it is not approved.
3. **Late changes:** any change inside 2 hours of send → the requester updates directly in the ESP, or the email is postponed.
4. **Ownership clarity:** every email has exactly 1 Owner (accountable) and 1 Reviewer (approver).
5. **No shadow emails.** Nothing built outside Airtable, sent without a record, or executed ad hoc.

**Scope:** all promotional, campaign, lifecycle, automated and broadcast email — anything that influences revenue or engagement. Exempt: pure transactional, cohort logistics, support comms. **If unclear, include it.**

**Exceptions** require explicit approval from the email lead, and only for genuine revenue/user emergencies.

**Enforcement:** emails sent outside this process get flagged, reviewed retroactively, and can cost the sender their execution access.

**Why it exists:** email drives a major share of company revenue. Poor execution is direct revenue loss. Inconsistent messaging is brand dilution. No process means burnout and chaos.

> **Zin — the hardest part of this job is not writing. It is holding this line when a senior stakeholder wants to skip it.** The SOP is what makes that a process decision rather than a personal one. Use it.

## 8. Performance: documenting, reading and reporting

### 8.1 How we DOCUMENT performance

**The master tracker: "Email Calendar & Performance" (Google Sheet).** Every send gets one row per segment. Columns:

`Date · Day · Campaign · Sequence · Email name · Send time (PST) · Segment · Suppression · UTM link · Braze campaign link · Subject line · Sends · Delivered · Delivery % · Opens · Clicks · CTR · CTOR · Leads · Lead %`

Rules that keep it trustworthy:
- **One row per segment**, not per email. A newsletter to 9 segments is 9 rows.
- Fill the Braze campaign link — it's how anyone re-derives your numbers.
- Log sends **before** they go out (from the calendar) and backfill results after.
- Campaign-level rollups live in per-campaign sheets, e.g. "Expert to Authority Summit Sept 2026 — leads breakdown".

The same workbook holds the Management tab (OKRs, GIMs, Key Initiatives, action steps), the Lifecycle journeys table, and the Benchmarks tab.

### 8.2 How we READ performance

**Step 1 — compare against the right benchmark, by sequence type.**

| Sequence | Target OR | Target CTR | Target CTOR | Max unsub | Current actual |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Invite** | 35–40% | 0.8–1.2% | 2.5–3.5% | 0.09% | 32.89% / 1.17% / 3.00% / 0.09% |
| **Show-up** | 40–50% | 5–8% | 12–18% | 0.08% | 45.22% / 7.78% / 15.50% / 0.12% |
| **Sales** | 38–45% | 1.5–3% | 4–6% | 0.13% | 41.49% / 2.52% / 5.49% / 0.16% |
| **Overall benchmark** | 35.92% | 2.81% | 5.48% | 0.10% | — |

Hitting these means you met standard expectations. It does not mean you won.

**Step 2 — read the metrics in the right order.**

| Metric | What it actually tells you | If it's low, fix… |
|---|---|---|
| **Delivery %** | List health, sender reputation | suppression hygiene, list cleaning — escalate below ~98% |
| **Open rate** | The subject line **and the list temperature** | subject line, send time, segment choice |
| **CTR** (clicks/delivered) | Whole-email effectiveness | the offer, the CTA |
| **CTOR** (clicks/opens) | **Body copy quality.** The purest read on your writing. | the story, the bridge, the CTA framing |
| **Leads** | The actual job on invite sequences | landing page match, CTA clarity |
| **Revenue** | The actual job on sales sequences | offer, timing, segment |
| **Unsub rate** | Whether you're burning the list | frequency, relevance, suppression |

**CTOR is the metric to obsess over.** OR is largely the subject line and the list. CTOR is your copy, isolated.

**Step 3 — always ask "compared to what?"** Structural differences distort everything. Real example from this month: the Expert to Authority Email 1 looked far bigger than the Speaking & Influence equivalent (~5.2M vs ~2.1M sends) — but only because EAS Email 1 was a *combined* send (the Sep 7 announcement across 6 segments **plus** the Sep 9 Vishen newsletter across 12 segments including Spanish, German, Events and VL). EAS won on reach; SIS won on engagement rate. Both true. Say both.

**Step 4 — diagnose, don't just describe.** The benchmark sheet models this well: cold lists at 13–14% OR are *expected* for awareness campaigns; broad unsegmented lists at 20–23% mean *segment further*; a just-announced summit at 22% means *the remind sequence hasn't run yet*. Name the cause, not just the number.

## 12. The 30 / 60 / 90 transition plan

The design principle: **Zin ships something real in week 1 and owns the highest-stakes recurring send by week 6.** Learning happens by doing under supervision, not by reading.

### Week 1 — Immersion (learn the language)
**Goal: understand the audience and the voice. Ship one small thing.**

- Vishen's high-performance session + Mindvalley culture onboarding. This is not optional fluff — the voice in the emails *is* this philosophy. She can't fake it without doing it.
- Read: this handbook, the Email Governance SOP, the master tracker.
- Read **3 full past sequences** end to end: Expert to Authority 2026 (invite + show-up), AI Summit 2025 invite, Speaking & Influence 2025 invite. Note what emails 1, 6 and 11 each do differently.
- Shadow: MOW, Campaign Weekly, one Daily Huddle, and — even though she won't own it — the Vishen Newsletter Sync. She's there for the voice, not the task.
- Set up all access (§11) and get Claude working with `mindvalley-email` loaded.
- **Ship:** draft 10 subject lines for a live campaign. Ramya picks and sends one. *She sees her words go to a real list in week 1.*

### Week 2 — Shadow the pipeline (learn the machine)
**Goal: know how an email physically gets out the door.**

- Walk one email through the full Airtable → review → Braze → QA → send flow, with Ramya narrating.
- Build one email in Braze herself, with Ramya doing the QA.
- Learn naming conventions and UTM/otag tagging by tagging 5 real emails and having them checked.
- Log a full week of sends into the master tracker.
- **Ship:** one complete lifecycle or broadcast email, written and built solo, reviewed by Ramya before send.

### Weeks 3–4 — Co-pilot (learn the craft)
**Goal: write to benchmark.**

- Write a full 3–4 email mini-sequence (a promo or a lifecycle journey), reviewed by Ramya.
- Own the weekly performance read: pull Metabase, fill the tracker, write the four-line read, present it at Campaign Weekly.
- Run the Friday MOW dashboard prep with Ramya.
- Start the daily Ranjini sync on international emails.
- **Checkpoint (end of week 4):** can she write an email that passes the pre-send checklist without edits? Can she read a result and say *why*, not just *what*?

### Weeks 5–8 — Lead with a net (learn the judgement)
**Goal: own the recurring sends.**

- **Owns the daily broadcast slot** end to end — planning, copy, build, send, and the numbers that come back. Ramya reviews but does not rewrite.
- **Owns one full campaign sequence** for the next launch — invite, show-up, sales. Ramya reviews each email before send.
- **Becomes the reviewer** for other teams' emails, with Ramya as second reviewer. *This is the hardest skill to transfer: saying "not yet, this doesn't meet the bar" to a senior stakeholder.* Practise it while Ramya is still there to back her up.
- Runs the MOW email dashboard solo.
- Takes over the Airtables & reporting sheets review.
- **Checkpoint (end of week 8):** run a full campaign retro herself.

### Weeks 9–12 — Own it (Ramya on call only)
**Goal: full ownership, Ramya as escalation only.**

- Owns the calendar, the governance process, the reviews, the reporting.
- Ramya moves to weekly 1:1 only. No pre-review of routine sends.
- Zin presents performance at Managers Weekly Marketing Update.
- **Final handover:** Zin updates this document with what she learned, and rewrites anything she found wrong or unclear. *That rewrite is the real proof she owns it.*

### The five things to over-invest in during handover

1. **Vishen's voice — and this gets harder, not easier, because Ramya keeps the newsletter.** The newsletter would have been the natural apprenticeship in that voice; without it, exposure has to be engineered deliberately. Get Zin into the newsletter syncs and strategy deep dives as an observer from week 1, have her read every newsletter draft before it ships, and send her the raw Vishen transcripts. The voice is caught, not taught, and she now has fewer chances to catch it.
2. **The relationships.** Personally introduce her to Monique, Ranjini, Sadaf, Glen, Marisha and the BI team — with the specific reason she'll need each of them. A warm intro saves her a month.
3. **The judgement calls, not the rules.** Rules are in this doc. What isn't: when to break the one-CTA rule, when a send is worth delaying, when to push back on Vishen. Narrate your reasoning out loud for 60 days — *"here's why I'm saying no to this"* — so she inherits the thinking, not just the process.
4. **The authority to say no.** Publicly hand over the reviewer role in a group setting so the org sees the transfer. Governance only works if people know who holds it.
5. **The failure library.** Walk her through the losers, not just the winners: the −64% social proof email, the listicle that missed the avatar, the reused subject line. Knowing what has already failed is worth more than knowing what worked.
