# Market sophistication pass · pawlingo 9-email sequence

Eugene Schwartz's five levels of awareness, applied to the welcome sequence after the first draft. Method from the Market Sophistication skill (Vishen Lakhiani, Expert Authority Summit 2026).

## Diagnosis

**Who gets these emails.** People who have already downloaded Pawlingo and typed in their dog's name. Tom has googled "why does my dog stare at me" at 1am and deleted three training apps by day four.

**Where the market actually is.**

| Relative to | Level | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The problem | 2 to 3 | Tom knows the pain ("I just wish I knew what he thought about it") and knows apps exist. He has been burned by three. |
| The free lessons | 4, Product Aware | He has the app. The job is to get him to open it, not to persuade him the problem exists. |
| Pawlingo Plus | 3, Solution Aware, moving to 4 | He knows Pawlingo. He does not yet know Plus is a different thing from the three apps he deleted. |

**Where the first draft was speaking.**

- Email 1 opened "He's heard everything" and spent three paragraphs on the secret-keeper insight before the lesson. That is Level 1 to 2 copy (name the hidden pain) sent to a Level 4 reader who acted ten seconds ago. It re-sells a problem he has already paid attention to and delays the one thing a Product Aware reader wants: the product.
- Email 5 introduced Plus as "the other half" with a benefit list. Benefit-led copy in a Level 3 moment reads like every other app in his deleted folder. Schwartz's flip: a mature, burned market needs the mechanism, not the transformation.
- Emails 2 to 4, 6 to 9 were already close to Level 4 (specific, demonstrated, easy next step) and needed sharpening rather than restructuring.

**The gap and its cost.** Level 1 to 2 openings on a Level 4 reader lower first-lesson completion, which is the number every later email depends on. A benefit-led Plus invitation on a Level 3 reader gets filed with the training apps. Both cost trial starts.

## Named mechanism

Level 3 copy needs a named method. Pawlingo had named features (Live Translate, breed packs) but no name for the method itself.

**The method already in the offer.** The dog is the teacher and the human is the one in training. Five-minute daily lessons on real body language (ears, tail, sigh, side-eye, blink), with breed-specific dialect packs. Nothing below invents a step the product does not contain.

| # | Pattern | Name | Describes | Ownable because |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Numbered | **The Three-Word Method** | Ears, tail, sigh: the first three "words" every owner learns | Sits directly on the deck's "Three words of Labrador before your coffee's cold" |
| 2 | Acronym | **S.I.T.** (Spot it, Interpret it, Talk back) | The three steps of each lesson | The dog telling the human to sit is the brand joke in one word; slightly forced |
| 3 | Origin | **The Professor Alsatian Method** | Lessons hosted by the character | Owned by the character; weaker at explaining the how |
| 4 | Metaphor | **Subtitles** | The app is the subtitles, the dog is the film | Concrete, deniable, already in email 4's copy |
| 5 | Contrarian verb | **Reverse Training** | The dog trains the human; no command is ever taught to the dog | Flips the entire category Tom has been burned by; sits on the approved hook "You've been training the wrong one" |

**Recommended: Reverse Training.** It differentiates in one breath against the three apps in his deleted folder, which is exactly what Level 3 copy has to do. It is two words, says what happens, and has no hype word in it. Used in emails 1, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 9.

Brand check: the brand file bans "train your dog." Reverse Training never asks the reader to train the dog; the dog is still the teacher. If the brand team vetoes the word on sight, The Three-Word Method is the drop-in replacement and the copy still holds. Subtitles stays as a supporting image in email 4.

## Rewrite: the two emails that moved level

### Email 1 (Level 1 to Level 4)

BEFORE
- Subject: {{dog_name}} has been trying to tell you something
- Headline: He's heard everything.
- Opening: The work calls. The 1am search for "why does my dog stare at me." The thing you said about your boss while you were on mute. {{dog_name}} heard all of it. He's never told a soul. He can't. Yet. Pawlingo is where you start listening back…

AFTER
- Subject: {{dog_name}}'s first lesson is in. He's already judging your accent.
- Headline: Lesson one: the sigh.
- Opening: You downloaded Pawlingo. {{dog_name}} noticed. He notices everything, which is rather the point. Here's how this works. He's the teacher. You're the one in training. We call it Reverse Training: five minutes a day of real dog, taught by the dog, and at no point does he learn a command. He's got enough of those. Lesson one is the sigh. It's in this email as well as in the app, so you can use it tonight.

What changed and why
- The subject now says what is in the email (the lesson) instead of hinting at a problem. Level 4 readers open for the product.
- The headline is the lesson, not the insight. The insight ("he's heard every word you've said for years") moved to one line at the end, as a bridge, where it earns the sign-off instead of delaying the lesson.
- The mechanism is named in paragraph two so the reader can file Pawlingo as a different category from the apps he deleted.
- Added "Tonight's homework": a specific, time-bound thing to do with the dog. Specificity and a next step are the Level 4 levers.
- Added the streak ("Day one starts when you tap. He's counting.") as the urgency a Level 4 reader responds to. Real urgency, not a fake deadline.

### Email 5 (Level 2 to Level 3)

BEFORE
- Preview: He's fluent in human. You're five days into dog. Here's the other half.
- Bridge: Here's what nobody says out loud. Every other app on your phone wanted to teach him… the fix was never him learning more of you. It's you learning him.
- Plus: Pawlingo Plus is the other half. Live Translate, which is for laughs, and every breed pack, which is for real.

AFTER
- Preview: Three apps tried to teach him. This is the one that teaches you. Here's the full syllabus.
- Bridge: Here's the difference between Pawlingo and the three apps you deleted. They taught him. Sit, stay, heel, obey. One way. You talk, you guess, he sighs. Pawlingo runs the other way, which is why we call it Reverse Training: he's the teacher, you've got the homework, and every lesson stops at five minutes so you go and find the actual dog.
- Plus: Pawlingo Plus is the full syllabus. Every breed pack, which is for real: Labrador, Dalmatian, Chihuahua, Alsatian and Golden Retriever, each with its own accent. And Live Translate, which is for laughs.

What changed and why
- Names the three deleted apps as the comparison point. Level 3 copy differentiates against the solutions the reader already knows.
- Names the mechanism and states its rule (five minutes, then go find the dog) so the difference is a process, not a mood.
- "The other half" became "the full syllabus." Syllabus is a mechanism word; it tells him what Plus contains, not how it feels.
- Lists the five breed packs by name. Specific beats "every breed pack."
- Day five's lesson (the side-eye) is now in the email, so the free product keeps delivering in the same email that invites him to pay.

## Sharpening on the Level 4 emails

- Email 2: alt subject now a task ("Check {{dog_name}} mid-sentence this morning"). Closing line sets up a demonstration: read him before he reads you.
- Email 3: added "Try it now. Say 'dinner' once… the ears go first." A live demonstration is the proof a Level 4 reader can run in ten seconds. Alt subject carries the same task.
- Email 4: the five-minute rule is now stated as the Reverse Training rule with the subtitles image. The tails lesson gets a specific promise: you'll see the stiff one for the first time.
- Email 6: headline became "Four things Biscuit said this week." Specific and countable. The innuendo line stays in the body.
- Email 7: breed packs framed as "Reverse Training in {{dog_name}}'s own accent." Alt subject is the Chihuahua-vs-Labrador demonstration.
- Email 8: the cancel path is now literal ("Settings, then Cancel. Done. No third screen.").
- Email 9: recap opens with "Two weeks of Reverse Training" and the deadline is a time, not a day.

## The full awareness ladder

For a single Pawlingo headline, one per level. Useful for ads, the landing page and any future sequence aimed earlier in the funnel than this one.

| Level | Headline |
|---|---|
| 1, Unaware | That sigh? It was about you. |
| 2, Problem Aware | You tell him everything. You understand about half of what comes back. |
| 3, Solution Aware | Reverse Training. Five minutes a day. He's the teacher, you're the one in training. |
| 4, Product Aware | {{dog_name}}'s first lesson is in. It's the sigh. Five minutes, starts now. |
| 5, Most Aware | Every breed, every dialect, Live Translate. Plus for £29.99 a year until 31 January. |

The welcome sequence lives at Levels 4 and 3. Levels 1 and 2 belong on the ads and the landing page, before the signup form.
