The Fastest Year in Mindvalley's History

Last Thursday, I was on a call with my team about YouTube channels.

Halfway through, I said, "Give me ten minutes. I'm uploading the new meditation app to TestFlight."

A few people laughed. They thought I was joking.

I wasn't.

I turned 50 this year. And apparently, when men turn 50, there are three traditional options. Buy a Ferrari. Buy a motorcycle. Or do something regrettable with your hair.

I chose a fourth option.

I started building.

And I want to tell you what I've been building, and why. Because this has been the fastest year of development in Mindvalley's 23-year history, and almost all of it is for you.

A promise I made a long time ago

Years ago, I wrote down a number on a whiteboard in our office in Kuala Lumpur.

One billion.

That's how many lives I want Mindvalley to touch. Not to sell to. To change.

And for a long time, I'll be honest with you, that number felt more like a prayer than a plan.

Here's why.

Personal growth has always had a delivery problem. We give you a brilliant teacher, a beautiful program, a powerful idea. And then life happens. The kids need breakfast. The inbox explodes. The program sits in your library, 40% complete, quietly judging you.

I know because I've done it too.

To reach a billion people, growth can't be an event you attend. It has to become a companion that walks with you. Something that knows you, meets you at 7am when you have exactly nine minutes, and gets wiser about you every single day.

For most of our history, building that was simply too expensive.

A few years ago, one of our apps cost us $600,000 and close to 50 developers.

This year, we rebuilt it with AI in one month, for $20,000.

And last month, standing in a terrible New York hotel gym, I built a working fitness app in eight hours, using nothing but WhatsApp on my iPhone.

The cost of turning an idea into something real has collapsed.

So we're doing what any company with a ridiculous mission should do when that happens.

We're building everything we always wished existed.

What's on my workbench right now

1. A meditation that knows how many minutes you have.

Most meditation apps hand you a library of thousands of tracks and wish you luck.

Our new meditation app, Omvana, starts with a simpler question: How many minutes do you have today?

Then it picks one meditation for you and tells you why.

I rebuilt the recommendation engine myself one afternoon. It draws on what years of Mindvalley listening have taught us about which meditations actually help which people. And for those of you who love it, it goes deeper: numerology, human design and moon phases, woven into what it suggests.

I've also argued with my team about the design of the timer more times than I'd like to admit. Some things matter.

2. A health companion that finally connects the dots.

Last Black Friday, I told you about yogurt.

Remember? I'd built myself a little AI food tracker. On Day 5 it told me I was severely deficient in fibre. I added one tiny habit, yogurt with chia, psyllium and berries, and started losing a centimetre off my waist every week.

That experiment didn't stay an experiment.

It became Vibrantly: a personal health companion that understands your meals, your bloodwork and your body in one place. You can ask questions of an AI that actually knows you.

3. A body with a CEO.

Coming soon is Body CEO, an AI personal trainer and health guide shaped by teachers like Ben Greenfield and Gary Brecka. It's built for people who want to stay strong, lean and full of energy for decades, not just until January.

4. EVE, who remembers your journey.

EVE is our AI companion inside Mindvalley. Ask her for a meditation, a program, a way through a hard week. She knows the library better than any of us, and she's learning more every day.

5. And the one thing AI will never replace: each other.

Here's what surprised me most this year.

The more technology we build, the more I realise humans want humans. That's why we keep pouring into our community, Finer Minds, and into gatherings like Mindvalley University. The AI handles the friction, so that you can spend more of your life in rooms full of people who want to grow.

This is the thing I want you to understand.

AI first means human first.

None of this exists to keep you on your phone. It exists to give you back the time, energy and clarity to live the life you actually came here for.

Why I'm telling you this

Personal growth has changed more in the last two years than in the twenty before it.

It used to be: read the book, go to the seminar, feel the high, crash on Monday.

Now it can be daily. Personal. With you at 7am, and still with you at 11pm.

I want Mindvalley to be the place where that future gets built responsibly. It should be built with teachers who've spent their lives on this work, not just algorithms that want your attention.

And honestly? Building it has made me feel 25 again.

One billion lives. For years I said it as a dream.

This year, for the first time, I can see the road.

An invitation

Once a year, at Black Friday, I get to hold the door open a little wider.

I won't tell you it's the deal of the century. It's simply the lowest price we offer all year, and the one time I can take price out of the conversation.

  • Everything new that we add to Membership as we keep building

And when you renew, your price stays at 60% off for as long as you remain a member.
